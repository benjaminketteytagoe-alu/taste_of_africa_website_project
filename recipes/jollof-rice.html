Ghanaian/Nigerian Jollof Rice

⏱ Prep: 20 min 🍳 Cook: 45 min Medium

Servings: 6-8 people

Cultural History & Significance

Jollof rice is one of the most iconic dishes in West African cuisine, with a heated and loving rivalry between Nigeria and Ghana over who makes it best. Originating from the Wolof people of Senegal, the dish spread across the West African coast through trade routes and colonial connections. Today, it is the centerpiece of celebrations, parties, and family gatherings across the region.

The dish is characterized by its vibrant red-orange color from the tomato and pepper base, with each family adding their own secret blend of spices. The smoky flavor achieved through the rice cooking near the heat source gives it its distinctive taste that has made it a global phenomenon.

Cooking Steps

Phase 1: Ingredient Prep & Marinade
  1. Rinse the long-grain rice thoroughly in cold water until the water runs clear. Drain and set aside.
  2. Blend tomatoes, red bell peppers, scotch bonnet peppers, onions, and garlic into a smooth puree.
  3. Chop additional onions for sautéing and mince fresh garlic and ginger.
  4. Prepare the stock by dissolving bouillon cubes in hot water.
Phase 2: Sauce Base & Browning
  1. Heat vegetable oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add chopped onions and sauté until golden.
  2. Pour in the blended tomato puree and cook for 15-20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce reduces and darkens.
  3. Add curry powder, thyme, cayenne pepper, and salt. Stir well and cook for another 5 minutes.
  4. The sauce should be thick and rich, with oil separating at the edges.
Phase 3: Simmering & Slow Cooking
  1. Add the rinsed rice to the sauce and stir to coat evenly with the tomato mixture.
  2. Pour in the hot stock gradually — the liquid should just cover the rice by about half an inch.
  3. Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low. Cover tightly with foil and then the lid to trap steam.
  4. Cook for 30-35 minutes without lifting the lid. The rice should absorb all the liquid and become tender.
  5. For the signature smoky flavor, let the bottom layer caramelize slightly in the last 5 minutes.
Phase 4: Plating & Traditional Accompaniments
  1. Fluff the rice gently with a fork, mixing the slightly burnt bottom layer (socarrat) into the rice.
  2. Plate the jollof rice and serve with fried plantains, grilled chicken, or fried fish.
  3. Garnish with sliced fresh tomatoes, onions, and hot pepper sauce on the side.
  4. Traditionally served with a side of salad or coleslaw for contrast.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups long-grain parboiled rice
  • 4 large ripe tomatoes
  • 2 red bell peppers
  • 2-3 scotch bonnet peppers (to taste)
  • 2 large onions (1 blended, 1 chopped)
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 inch fresh ginger, grated
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 3 cups chicken or vegetable stock
  • 2 tablespoons curry powder
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1 teaspoon cayenne pepper
  • 2 bouillon cubes
  • Salt and black pepper to taste
  • Bay leaves (optional)

Equipment

  1. Large heavy-bottomed pot with tight-fitting lid
  2. Blender or food processor
  3. Wooden spoon for stirring
  4. Measuring cups and spoons
  5. Knife and cutting board
  6. Aluminum foil (for trapping steam)
